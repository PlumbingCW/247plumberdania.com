Sewer Camera Inspection in Dania, FL

By the Dania 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated October 11, 2026.

A sewer camera inspection runs a waterproof camera through your main sewer line so you can see cracks, roots, offsets and buildup on screen. For Dania Beach homeowners it replaces guesswork with footage of the actual pipe.

Call (954) 697-4920

What a Sewer Scope Shows That a Cable Never Will

A cable tells you where it stopped. A camera tells you why. The head sits on a flexible push rod, carries its own lights, and sends live video up the line from the 4-inch cleanout while we watch for what the cable cannot report.

That difference matters most when a drain keeps clogging after repeated visits. Instead of a third round of cabling, the footage can show a root mass at a joint, a belly where water pools, or a clean pipe that points the problem at the street main.

Reading the Footage: What We Look For

We pause the video at each defect and note how far from the cleanout it sits. The table lists what the screen can show and what each finding usually means.

Buying or Selling a Dania Beach Home? Scope the Lateral

A 1977 median build year and a $319,800 median owner-occupied value, both from the ACS, mean many Dania Beach purchases pair an aging pipe with a large price. A sewer problem found after closing can cost far more than the camera visit before it. A general home inspection may not include the sewer line, so ask your inspector what it covers.

Angi's pipe guide says clay sewer pipe averages 50 to 60 years and is vulnerable to roots, while cast iron is listed at about 100 years but corrodes and clogs. No Broward survey shows which of those, if either, sits under a given lot. The footage answers that for your address.

The Gear and How the Visit Goes

We open the cleanout, feed the camera head down the line, and watch the screen while a counter tracks the distance. If a locator is needed, a transmitter in the camera head lets us mark the pipe's depth and path above ground. We record the run so you can review it later and show a seller, a buyer, a landlord or the city.

If the footage shows heavy grease or scale, we say whether hydro jetting at high PSI is safe for that pipe or whether the pipe is too weak. If it shows a break, we explain the choices in sewer line repair and do not push one.

What to Check First Before the Camera Visit

A few minutes of preparation saves time on site.

  • Find the cleanout, a capped pipe about 4 inches wide near the house wall or yard edge.
  • Clear leaves, mulch and furniture around it so we can open it safely.
  • Write down the symptoms: slow drains, gurgling, smells, wet patches, and the dates they started.
  • Gather any earlier plumbing reports, plans or permits for the sewer line.
  • If you plan to dig anywhere after the inspection, call Sunshine 811 first, since notice is required at least two full business days before digging.

Apartments, Rentals and Mobile Homes

About 12.4 percent of Dania Beach housing units are mobile homes, and 1,860 units sit in buildings of 50 or more. Different building types put the sewer line in different places. We ask where the cleanout is, which units are affected and who holds the keys before we schedule.

Landlords and managers get footage and a short written description of each defect, which helps when more than one party shares the cost. We also work in Pembroke Park and West Park, so a manager with buildings across the area can use one contact.

Honest Limits of a Camera Inspection

A camera sees the inside of the pipe, not the soil around it. It cannot tell you what a repair will cost until the length and depth are measured. We cannot tell by phone who owns the stretch from your lot to the street, so ask the Dania Beach sewer office with the footage in hand.

If the line is blocked solid, the camera cannot pass. In that case we clear it first, usually with sewer line cleaning, and then run the camera. If you see sewage in a yard or under a floor, Call (954) 697-4920 and treat it as urgent.

What a Sewer Camera Inspection Costs

National published guides vary widely. HomeAdvisor lists $271 to $1,725 for a sewer inspection, while This Old House puts it at $100 to $500. Angi's Tampa page, the nearest Florida figure we found, lists $216 to $907. Florida prices vary and we found no Broward figure.

What moves the price

  • Line length. A long run from the back of the lot takes more time than a short one.
  • Cleaning first. A line packed with grease or debris must be cleared before the camera passes.
  • Locating. Marking the pipe's path and depth above ground adds time.
  • Recording and report. Footage with notes takes longer than a quick look.
  • Access. A missing cleanout may need a fixture pulled to get in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a sewer camera inspection worth it before buying a house in Dania?

If the home is older, yes. The median Dania Beach home dates to 1977, and a hidden lateral problem can cost more than the inspection. The footage also gives you something concrete to bring to the negotiation.

Can you inspect a line that is completely blocked?

Not until it is cleared. We open the line first, then run the camera, and tell you what the footage shows after the blockage is gone.

Will the camera damage my pipes?

No. The head is smooth and rides on a flexible rod. A fragile pipe can crumble under a cable or a jetter, which is one reason the camera should go first.

Do I get a copy of the video?

We record the run and can describe each defect with its distance from the cleanout. Ask for the footage when you book so we set the recording up.

I manage rentals. Can you scope a shared line?

Yes. Tell us which units are affected and where the cleanout sits. We scope the shared line and give you a clear description for owners or insurers.

How do I book?

Call (954) 697-4920 and give us the address, the symptoms and whether you have a cleanout. We schedule the visit and tell you what to prepare.
