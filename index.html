Plumbing Emergency in Dania? Do This Before You Call

Close the main water valve first. It is usually near the water heater, at the front wall or by the meter, and turning it clockwise stops the flow into the house.

Switch off power to any room with standing water and stay clear of outlets. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 first, then the gas utility. Do not use a phone or light switch inside.

Then call our 24/7 line. We cannot promise an arrival time before we know the problem, so you get an honest estimate when we pick up.

Before We Arrive

Shut the main

Turn the main valve clockwise until it stops. Do not force a stuck valve.

Cut power near water

Flip the breaker for wet rooms and the water heater if it is leaking.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and anything stored under sinks or beside the heater.

Note the meter

Photograph the meter dial. It helps us tell an outside leak from an inside one.

Dania Beach runs its own water system. It draws from the Biscayne aquifer, treats the water with lime softening and nanofiltration, then adds chlorine. The city's 2024 water report does not publish a hardness number, so we do not guess one. If a fixture scales up, we look at the fixture.

The median home dates to 1977. Original drain lines, shutoff valves and supply fittings behind the walls may still be in place, so we check them whenever a wall is already open. Our repiping page explains when patching stops making sense.

Wastewater from Dania Beach flows to the City of Hollywood's Southern Regional plant. When a Hollywood plant pipe failed in November 2023, customers were asked to cut water use. A backup during a storm can be a street problem instead of a house problem, and a camera run tells the two apart. See sewer inspection.

What Plumbing Repairs Cost in Dania, FL

We do not post fixed prices, because the job decides the price. National published guides give the ranges in the table, and Florida prices vary.

What moves the number is access, pipe condition, the time of day and whether a permit is needed. Permits follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). A camera look or a meter test usually settles the scope before any digging.
