Leaky Bathroom Faucet Repair in Dania, FL

By the Dania 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated October 11, 2026.

A dripping bathroom faucet in Dania, FL almost always comes down to one worn part: a cartridge, a rubber seal or a valve seat. Close the two stop valves under the sink, swap that part, and the drip usually stops without buying a new faucet.

First, which kind of faucet do you have?

Repairs differ by design, so identify yours before you buy parts. Pull off the handle cap and look at what sits underneath. A single lever that swings left and right for temperature almost always hides a cartridge or a ceramic disc unit. Two separate knobs usually mean compression washers or ceramic discs.

Ball faucets are rare in bathrooms and far more common at kitchen sinks. If your bathroom faucet has a rounded cap under the handle and a slotted metal ball inside, you have one. The table below matches each type to its usual drip.

Why a faucet keeps dripping after you tighten it

Cranking a handle harder rarely cures a drip. It squeezes a worn washer or cracked O-ring against a seat that is already scratched, and that damage gets worse. The rubber parts harden with age, and tiny mineral flakes can score a brass seat.

A drip is also not harmless. The EPA WaterSense program says a faucet dripping once per second wastes more than 3,000 gallons a year, which equals over 180 showers. That adds up quickly on a household bill.

Cartridge repair, step by step

Cartridge faucets are the most common bathroom type, so this is the repair most people need. You need an adjustable wrench, a small flat screwdriver, a hex key set, a rag and the exact replacement cartridge. Bring the old cartridge to the store, because lookalikes with the wrong spline count will not seat properly.

  1. Close both stop valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If they will not budge or they drip when turned, stop and close the home's main shutoff instead.
  2. Open the faucet to drain the lines and plug the drain with a rag so no screw falls in.
  3. Pry off the handle cap, remove the handle screw, and lift the handle away.
  4. Remove the retaining clip or nut with pliers, then pull the cartridge straight up, rocking it gently. Note how the stem faces before it comes out.
  5. Wipe the cartridge cavity clean, push the new cartridge in with the same orientation, and replace the clip.
  6. Rebuild the handle, open the stop valves slowly, and run both hot and cold for a minute to flush debris.

Ball and ceramic disc repairs

On a ball faucet, the fix is usually a replacement kit with new seats, springs and a cam washer. Take photos as you disassemble, because the parts nest in a set order and the ball has a small notch that must line up with a pin in the body.

Ceramic disc faucets are tougher and often last longer. When one drips, the disc cylinder or its rubber seals have usually worn, and a new cylinder costs little. Tighten the retaining screws only until snug, since the polished discs can crack.

Water under the sink or at the wall is a different problem

If the drip is from the supply lines, the stop valves or the drain tailpiece, a new cartridge will not help. Dry each connection with a paper towel and watch which one beads up first. A damp cabinet floor, a swollen particleboard base or a musty smell points to a slow leak that has been running for a while.

Some jobs should go to a plumber. Corroded stop valves that crumble or will not turn, a stripped faucet body, or a leak that continues after a new cartridge all fall in that group. Our faucet repair service handles those, and leak detection helps when you cannot find where the moisture starts.

  • Stop valve will not close fully or leaks at its stem
  • Handle spins freely and the faucet body is cracked or pitted
  • Water appears under the cabinet but not at the faucet
  • The drip returns within days of a new cartridge
  • Water drips from a ceiling light or outlet below the bathroom (kill that breaker and call right away)

Repair or replace the whole faucet?

Repairing is the better choice when the finish is sound and parts are available. Replace the faucet when the body is pitted, the manufacturer no longer sells cartridges, or you want a different style anyway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I fix a leaky bathroom faucet without turning off the main water?

Yes, if the two stop valves under the sink close fully. Open the faucet after closing them to confirm the flow stops. If a stop valve will not turn or keeps dripping, close the main shutoff to the house instead.

How do I know whether to replace the cartridge or the whole faucet?

Replace the cartridge if the faucet body and finish look sound and the part number is still sold. Replace the whole faucet if the body is pitted, the handle is loose on a cracked base, or no cartridge is available.

Why does my faucet still drip after I changed the cartridge?

The most common causes are a scratched valve seat, a cartridge with the wrong orientation, or a missed O-ring. A leak at the supply line or drain can also look like a faucet drip. Dry each part and watch where water appears first.

Does the city's softened water mean my faucet seals last longer in Dania Beach?

We cannot promise that. The city softens its water, but its report does not publish a hardness number, and seals also age from chlorine and heat. Judge by what you find inside the faucet when you open it.

When should I call a plumber for a bathroom faucet?

Call when a stop valve will not close, when water shows under the cabinet, or when a repair fails twice. A plumber can also check for a hidden supply leak. Reach us any hour at Call (954) 697-4920.
