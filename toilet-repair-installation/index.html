Emergency Toilet Repair in Dania, FL

By the Dania 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated October 11, 2026.

Toilet repair in Dania, FL means fixing whatever keeps a toilet from flushing, filling or sealing to the floor, from a worn flapper to a cracked closet flange. We work across Dania Beach on running toilets, leaking bases and overflows, and we replace the whole toilet when a repair no longer makes sense.

Toilet overflowing right now? Stop the water first

A toilet spilling onto the bathroom floor is an emergency, and our dispatch line is open 24/7 for Dania Beach. While you wait, reach behind the bowl and turn the oval supply valve clockwise until it stops. That cuts off the tank refill and usually ends the flood within seconds.

If the valve will not turn, lift the lid and raise the float so the fill valve closes. Do not flush again. Stay out of standing water if any outlet or power strip sits near the floor.

When water also rises in the tub or a floor drain, the clog is past the toilet, in the branch or main line. Stop using every fixture and say so on the call, because that is a sewer line cleaning job and not a bowl repair.

Water around the base of the toilet

Water on the floor at the base of a toilet comes from one of a few places, and the wax ring is only one of them. Before anyone pulls the bowl, we check the supply line, the tank-to-bowl bolts, the shutoff valve stem and the seal at the floor. Dry everything with a towel and watch which spot turns wet first.

The pattern tells us what to load on the truck. A floor that is wet only after flushing points down, to the seal. A floor that is wet all day points up, to the tank or the supply.

Why the toilet runs on its own and refills every few minutes

A toilet that runs by itself is nearly always the flapper or the fill valve. The flapper is the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. When it warps, water creeps into the bowl and the fill valve tops the tank off again, which is the sound you hear at 2 a.m.

EPA WaterSense tells homeowners to replace toilet flappers at least every five years. Its dye test is simple: add food coloring to the tank, wait 10 minutes without flushing, and look for color in the bowl. Color means the flapper is leaking.

The stakes are real. EPA reports that the average household leaks more than 9,300 gallons of water a year, and fixing the easy leaks can cut a water bill by about 10 percent. If the dye stays in the tank but the bill keeps climbing, we look further, and leak detection covers the lines you cannot see.

What to check first before you call

Two minutes of looking helps us bring the right parts the first time.

  • Jiggle the handle. If the running stops, the chain or flapper is sticking.
  • Look for the chain caught under the flapper or hanging too long.
  • Lift the lid and see whether water spills into the overflow tube. If it does, the fill valve is not shutting off.
  • Run the dye test and wait the full 10 minutes.
  • Check that the oval supply valve behind the bowl turns by hand.
  • Note whether the bowl is round or elongated and read the brand off the tank or the bowl rim.

Clogged toilet first aid before the plumber gets there

Most bowl clogs clear with a flange plunger, the kind with a rubber sleeve that folds out of the cup. A flat cup plunger seals poorly on a toilet drain. Go slowly, because splashing is the main way a clog turns into a cleanup. If the plunger fails and the water keeps rising, Call (954) 697-4920 before it reaches the rim.

  1. Stop flushing and close the supply valve so the tank cannot add water.
  2. Seat the flange plunger over the drain hole until it holds suction.
  3. Push gently, then pull up sharply, about 15 to 20 strokes.
  4. Pour a bucket of warm water, not boiling, since boiling water can crack porcelain.
  5. If the bowl drains, flush once and watch the level.
  6. If it backs up again, or other drains gurgle, stop and call. Further plunging will not reach a problem in the branch line.

Wax ring, flange and a toilet that rocks

The closet flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet bolts to, and it sits on top of the 3-inch or 4-inch drain. A wax ring, or a wax-free seal, fills the gap between that flange and the bowl so water and sewer gas stay in the pipe. When a toilet rocks, the seal breaks, and the first sign is often a faint odor or a stain on the ceiling below.

We pull the bowl, scrape off the old wax and check the flange for cracks, rust and height against the finished floor. A flange set too low after new tile will not compress the seal, so a spacer ring raises it. A broken flange gets a repair ring or a replacement, which is why a simple wax ring job sometimes grows.

Moving the toilet, cutting new drain pipe or opening a wall changes that answer. For those jobs, ask the City of Dania Beach Building Division what it needs before work starts. We will not guess on the phone.

Repair the old toilet or put in a new one

The median Dania Beach home was built in 1977 per the American Community Survey, so some toilets are old enough that matching parts are hard to find. A new fill valve and flapper are cheap. A cracked bowl, a hairline crack in the tank or a toilet that clogs every week is a different story.

A replacement visit covers shutting off the supply, removing the old bowl, setting a new seal and braided supply line, tightening the closet bolts evenly and testing the flush with a leak check.

Toilet repair price ranges from national guides

National published guides put toilet repair at about $150 to $391, and Florida prices vary. Replacing a toilet, labor included, runs about $224 to $533 in the same guides, with plumber-installed jobs listed from $300 to $800.

These are ranges from public cost guides, not our prices. The final figure depends on what we find under the bowl.

What moves the price

  • Part or whole fixture. A flapper or fill valve is a small part. A wax ring job adds pulling and resetting the bowl, and a replacement adds the toilet itself.
  • Flange or floor damage. A cracked flange or soft subfloor adds parts and sometimes a second trade.
  • Supply valve condition. A seized shutoff valve must be replaced before any work, and that adds a step.
  • Night or weekend call. Ask on the call whether nights, weekends or holidays are priced differently.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle toilet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Dania?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and an overflowing toilet is handled like any active leak. We cannot promise an arrival time by phone, but we will tell you what to shut off while the plumber is on the way. Call (954) 697-4920 and describe what the toilet is doing.

Why does my toilet run by itself, then refill every few minutes?

That phantom refill is usually a flapper that no longer seals, so water slips into the bowl and the fill valve restores the tank. Try the dye test. Color in the bowl after 10 minutes confirms it.

Is sewage coming up in the tub the same as a clogged toilet?

No. When the tub or a floor drain shows water as well, the blockage is further down the branch or main line. Stop using water and call, because plunging the bowl will not clear it.

Do I need a permit to replace a toilet in Dania Beach?

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Dania Beach Building Division about your address, especially if the toilet is moving or the drain pipe is changing.

I rent my home. Can I call about a toilet that is flooding?

You can call for an active overflow, and we will help you stop the water. Repair costs in a rental are normally approved by the owner or the property manager, so tell us on the call who to reach.

The toilet leak is staining the ceiling of the unit below. What now?

Close the supply valve, stop all water in your unit and call right away. More than 9,000 Dania Beach housing units are in buildings with two or more units, so a shared stack or neighbor is common. Tell us the unit numbers so the repair can be coordinated with your manager.

Can you replace a toilet fill valve or a broken flush lever?

Yes. Those parts wear out inside the tank and are a standard toilet repair. Tell us the make if you know it and whether the tank is one piece or two. If water is running onto the floor, close the oval supply valve behind the bowl first.
